# Thorns

Everyone hides roses and one thorn face down. Bid how many you can flip without pricking yourself, then prove it. 3–6 players.

## The short version

- You hold four discs: three roses and one thorn. Nobody else sees yours.
- Stack discs face down on your mat, then bid how many you can flip without hitting a thorn.
- The top bidder flips: their own stack first, then other people's top discs.
- All roses scores a point. Two points wins. A thorn costs you a disc, and only you find out which.

## How it plays

1. **Opening.** Everyone puts one disc face down on their own mat, all at once. Tap a disc in your hand to put it down. The round starts once everyone has.
2. **Turns.** Clockwise from the starting player. On your turn, either tap a disc to put it on top of your stack, or bid a number from 1 to the total discs on all mats. If your hand is empty, you have to bid.
3. **Bidding.** Once anyone bids, nobody places any more. Each player in turn raises (use the − / + stepper, then **Bid N**) or presses **Pass**. A pass is for the rest of the round.
4. Bidding ends when everyone else has passed, or someone bids every disc on the table.
5. **Flipping.** The top bidder's own stack turns over first, all of it, top down, by itself. Then they tap other players' mats (marked **Flip**) to turn the top disc of each, one at a time, in any order.
6. A thorn ends it at once. Reaching the bid on roses alone is a success.
7. **Result.** A few seconds to take it in, with a draining bar. Everyone can tap **Ready** to move on sooner. Then the discs go back to hands and the next round starts.
8. **Who starts next.** After a success, the challenger. After a thorn, its owner. If it was the challenger's own thorn, the challenger, or the next player along if they're now out.

## Winning and scoring

- A success scores one point. The first to two points wins (or three, if the host picked it).
- A failure costs the challenger one disc, picked at random from everything they own. Everyone sees their disc count drop; only they see whether it was a rose or their thorn. Losing your thorn means you can only play roses, and nobody knows.
- Down to zero discs and you're out. The last player with discs also wins.
- Everyone else is ranked by points, then discs left.

## On your screen

- **Mats.** One per player in a grid, you first, then clockwise. Each shows the stack (face-down discs, flipped ones face up), points as filled dots against the target, and four small dots for discs owned.
- **Your mat.** Your own discs are drawn face up but dimmed: only you see them.
- **Chips** above the mats: points to win, the round, discs on the mats, the standing bid and, while flipping, how many have turned.
- **Your hand.** At the bottom. Glowing discs can be tapped to place. The count reads "2 of 4 discs": in hand, out of everything you own.
- **Bid controls.** A − / + stepper, **Bid N** and, once someone has bid, **Pass**.
- **Flipping.** Each flip pauses for a beat. A thorn gets a sting: the mat shakes, the disc pops, and a heavier sound. Then a stamp: "All roses" or "Thorn!".
- **After a thorn.** If it was your challenge, the dock tells you which disc you lost. Nobody else's screen does.
- Sounds and vibration cue placing, bids, flips, your turn and the ending.

## When things go sideways

- **Idle player.** The host gets a "Skip" button after a while. A skip makes the least damaging move: in the opening or on a turn, a rose if they have one, otherwise whatever's left in their hand (face down, so nobody learns it was the thorn), and the lowest bid only when their hand is empty; while bidding, a pass, so a skipped bidder never wins the bid; while flipping, the tallest stack left.
- **Disconnected player.** The game waits on them like any turn; the mat shows "reconnecting…" and the host can Skip. The result's Ready count doesn't wait for them.
- **The host removes a player.** Their stack leaves the table. Mid-round (challenge included), the round doesn't count: discs go back to hands, nobody loses one, and the next player after them starts. During a result, the next round just starts without them. If only one player is left, they win.
- **Game over.** The host sees Rematch and Back to lobby; everyone else sees Play again.

## Tips

- Your own stack flips first, so a thorn of your own on the mat makes any bid you win a bad idea. That's also why it works as a bluff.
- Bidding every disc on the table ends the bidding at once: nobody gets to call you on it except the discs.
- Someone who's lost a disc might have lost their thorn. Or not. Their disc count is all you get.
