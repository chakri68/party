# Sevens

Build each suit out from the seven. First to empty their hand wins. 2–16 players.

## The short version

- Every card is dealt out. The goal is to get rid of yours first.
- Sevens start a row. Every other card goes down only next to what's already there, one rank at a time, up or down.
- If you can play, you must. If you can't, you pass automatically.
- First player with no cards left wins. By default there's no points tally, just the win.

Those are the defaults. The host can change who starts, where aces sit, whether you have to play, and how the losers are ranked (see [Settings](#settings)).

## How it plays

1. The deck is dealt clockwise, starting left of the dealer, until it's gone. Some hands end up a card bigger than others.
2. The player left of the dealer goes first. The board is empty, so the only thing anyone can play is a seven. No seven in your hand means you pass without being asked. With **First move** on *Seven of diamonds*, whoever holds the 7♦ goes first instead, and it has to be the first card down.
3. A seven starts its suit's row. After that, a card of that suit goes down only right next to the row's ends: a 6 or an 8 after the 7, then a 5 or a 9, and so on outward.
4. Aces are high. They go after the King, so the row for a suit runs 2 to Ace. With **Aces** on *Low*, they go before the 2 instead, and the row runs Ace to King.
5. On your turn, play any highlighted card. Anything that doesn't fit is dimmed.
6. If nothing fits, the game passes you and moves on. You'll see "You had nothing to play, so you passed." With **Passing** on *Any time*, nobody passes for you: press **Pass** when you're stuck, or whenever you'd rather hold a card back.
7. Play ends the moment somebody plays their last card.

With more than one deck, each suit gets one row per deck, and each seven can start a new row. A card goes to the first row it fits.

## Winning and scoring

The first player to empty their hand wins. Everyone else is ranked by how many cards they have left, fewest first.

With **Scoring** on *Penalty points*, the cards left are worth points instead: face value, courts 10, aces 1 (high or low). Everyone else ranks by points, lowest first, so two small cards beat one stranded King. It's still one hand; the points just settle second place onward.

Players who left go to the bottom either way, since their cards went down without them.

## Settings

Host picks these in the lobby.

| Setting | Options | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decks | **Auto**, 1 deck, 2 decks, 3 decks | How many decks are shuffled together. Auto uses 1 deck for up to 6 players, 2 for 7–12, 3 beyond. The lobby shows the cards each player will get. |
| First move | **Left of dealer**, Seven of diamonds | Who starts. Seven of diamonds hands the first turn to whoever holds it, and nothing else can go down until it does. |
| Aces | **High**, Low | Whether an Ace goes after the King or before the 2. |
| Passing | **Only when stuck**, Any time | Only when stuck: you must play if you can, and the game passes for you when you can't. Any time: you can pass with playable cards, and you press Pass yourself. |
| Scoring | **Winner only**, Penalty points | How everyone after the winner is ranked: by cards left, or by what they're worth (face value, courts 10, aces 1). |

## On your screen

- **The board.** One row per suit (and per deck). Played cards show up; empty slots are outlines, with the seven slot marked.
- **Other players.** Each shows their card count. A "D" marks the dealer, and the player whose turn it is is highlighted.
- **Your hand.** Playable cards are highlighted.
  - On a mouse, click a card to play it.
  - On touch, tap once to select, then tap again or press the **Play** button.
- **Pass.** Only appears when you're allowed to pass. On *Only when stuck* that's when nothing fits, which the game usually handles for you. On *Any time* it's there your whole turn, next to Play.
- **Status line.** Shows whose turn it is, or "Your turn". Tapping a card out of turn gets "Not your turn yet."
- Sounds and vibration cue the deal, plays, passes, your turn and the ending.

## When things go sideways

- **A player is idle or disconnected.** After a while the game offers a Nudge, and the host gets a "Skip" button. A skip passes that player's turn, even if they had a card to play.
- **The host removes a player.** Their cards go down by themselves, as soon as they fit, so the board never gets stuck. If only one player is left, they win.
- **Game over.** The host sees Rematch and Back to lobby; everyone else sees Play again.

## Tips

- Holding a seven early matters: nothing in that suit can move until someone opens it.
