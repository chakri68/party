# Cheat

Put cards down face down and say what they are. Lie if you have to. Get caught and the pile's yours. 3–10 players.

## The short version

- The whole deck is dealt. Get rid of your cards first.
- On your turn, put one to four cards face down and claim they're the rank that's up. You don't have to be telling the truth.
- After each play, anyone else can call "Cheat!" before the timer runs out.
- If the play was a lie, the player takes the whole pile. If it was true, the caller does.

## How it plays

1. The deck is dealt out, clockwise from the dealer's left. Some hands are a card bigger.
2. The player left of the dealer goes first. The first claim is always Aces, then Twos, Threes, up to Kings, then round to Aces again.
3. Pick one to four cards from your hand. You must claim the rank that's up, whatever you're holding. Press the button that reads "Put down N as <ranks>".
4. A call window opens for everyone else: a draining bar shows the time left. They each see "Cheat!" and "Let it go".
5. If someone presses **Cheat!** (first call wins), the cards flip for everyone, with a verdict of "Lie!" or "True". If any card isn't the claimed rank, the player who put them down takes the whole pile. If all were true, the caller takes it.
6. If nobody calls, the window closes when the timer runs out or when everyone has let it go, and the next player carries on. The pile keeps growing.
7. After a call, play moves to the player after the one who played, whoever took the pile.

## Winning and scoring

First to get rid of every card wins, as long as their last play survives. If their last play is called and it was a lie, they take the pile and keep playing. If the last play was true, they win, called or not. Everyone else is ranked by cards left.

## Settings

Host picks these in the lobby.

| Setting | Options | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decks | **Auto**, 1 deck, 2 decks | Auto uses 1 deck up to 6 players, 2 beyond. With two decks, holding four of a rank no longer proves a lie. The lobby shows cards per player. |
| Claims | **Next rank up**, Same, up or down one | Next rank up: every claim is the next rank in the sequence. Same, up or down one: you can repeat the last claim or go one either way (after Sevens: Sixes, Sevens or Eights). You pick which. |
| Time to call | 5 seconds, **8 seconds**, 12 seconds | How long everyone has to call after a play. It closes early once everyone's let it go. |

## On your screen

- **The pile.** A face-down stack with its count, and the last play: who, how many, and the claim ("one Seven", "three Sevens").
- **Your own play.** While it's on top, you can see the cards you actually put down.
- **Your hand.** Sorted by rank. Tap up to 4 cards to select them.
- **Claim buttons.** One button if only one rank is allowed. With the near rule, one button per rank.
- **During a window.** If you can call: **Let it go** and **Cheat!**. The play's owner sees "Straight face...". Players who let it go see "You let it go".
- **The clock.** A bar drains over the window's length. It ticks audibly near the end for anyone who can still call.
- **Reveal.** After a call, the cards flip and stay visible until the next play.
- Sounds and vibration cue the deal, plays, calls, your turn and the ending.

## When things go sideways

- **Calling late.** If someone else's call already settled the play, yours is refused as too late.
- **Disconnected player.** They can't call, so the window doesn't wait for them. If they were the last one who might have called, the play is accepted straight away. They can call again once they reconnect.
- **Idle player.** The host gets a "Skip" button after a while. A skip plays one card for them: a true one if they hold the claimable rank, otherwise a random card as a lie.
- **The host removes a player.** Their cards go under the pile. If they had the play on top, it can't be called any more and play moves on. If only one player is left, they win.
- **Game over.** The host sees Rematch and Back to lobby; everyone else sees Play again.

## Tips

- A play is capped at four cards, and four of a kind is the most anyone could honestly hold with one deck.
- The pile only grows until someone's wrong, so a wrong call late costs more than one early.
