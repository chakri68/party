# Teen Patti

Three cards each, and a pot that grows with every bet. Play Blind or look at your cards, bet or pack, until one player has every chip. 3–7 players, best with 4–6.

Chips are play chips: no buying, no cash-out, nothing carries over between games.

Computer players can fill seats in this game. They look at their cards early, bet on good hands and pack weak ones, and bluff now and then. They ask for a Show sooner when the pot is big, and they answer a Sideshow by their cards.

The host can pause this game. While paused, the turn timer and the Sideshow timer stop, and so does the time limit.

## The short version

- Everyone starts with the same chips. Run out and you are out.
- Each hand, everyone puts in the boot, and gets 3 cards face down.
- Bet Blind (without looking) for less, or look and bet Seen for more.
- Pack to give up. The last player in wins the pot, or the best hand when the cards turn over.
- The boot goes up every few hands, so the game always ends.

## The words

- **Boot:** the small bet everyone puts in before the cards come.
- **Blind:** you have not looked at your cards yet.
- **Seen:** you looked at your cards.
- **Stake:** the bet now. A Blind bet is the stake. A Seen bet is twice the stake.
- **Chaal:** a bet to stay in.
- **Pack:** give up this hand. The chips you put in stay in the pot.
- **Show:** with 2 players left to bet, every hand still in turns over and the best hand wins.
- **Sideshow:** you ask the player before you who is still betting to compare cards in private.

## How it plays

1. **The dealer** moves one player left each hand. Everyone puts in the boot. Cards go out one at a time, starting left of the dealer.
2. **Play starts** left of the dealer and goes round the table. There are no rounds of betting: it goes round and round until the hand ends.
3. **Everyone starts Blind.** Tap **See** at any time to look at your cards. Seeing is free. If it is your turn, you still play this turn.
4. **The stake** starts at the boot. On your turn:
   - **Pack:** give up this hand.
   - **Chaal:** bet 1 times the stake if you are Blind, or 2 times the stake if you are Seen.
   - **Double:** bet 2 times the stake if you are Blind, or 4 times the stake if you are Seen. The stake doubles for everyone. For example, the stake is 20. A Seen player doubles: they bet 80, and the stake is now 40. The next Blind bet is 40, and the next Seen bet is 80.
   - **Show** (only when 2 players are left to bet): pay your normal bet, and every hand still in turns over. Either player may ask for a Show, Blind or Seen. Each pays their own price: a Blind player pays the Blind price, a Seen player the Seen price. The best hand wins the pot. If your hand is equal to the hand of the player you asked, you lose.
   - **Sideshow** (only when 3 or more players are left to bet): if you are Seen, and the player before you who is still betting is also Seen, you can ask them for a Sideshow. It costs your normal Seen bet. They say yes or no. They have 15 seconds; no answer is a no.
     - **Yes:** you two see each other's cards. Nobody else does. The lower hand packs. If the hands are equal, the player who asked packs. Then play goes on from the player after you.
     - **No:** nothing happens. Your bet stays in the pot, and play goes on.
5. **The hand ends** when only one player is left (they win the pot without showing), after a Show, or when the cards turn over for one of the reasons below.

Each button shows its price, like "Chaal 40" or "Double 80". Every seat shows **Blind** or **Seen**, so you always know who has looked.

## Hand rankings

Best first:

1. **Trail:** 3 cards of the same rank, like 7-7-7. A-A-A is the best, 2-2-2 the lowest.
2. **Pure sequence:** 3 cards in a sequence, all in one suit.
3. **Sequence:** 3 cards in a sequence, in any suits.
4. **Color:** 3 cards of one suit, not in a sequence.
5. **Pair:** 2 cards of the same rank.
6. **High card:** none of these.

**Sequences (for both kinds):** A-K-Q is the best. A-2-3 is next. Then K-Q-J, Q-J-10, and so on down to 4-3-2. K-A-2 is not a sequence.

**Ties:** two hands of the same kind compare by rank. A Pair compares the pair first, then the third card. Color and High card compare the top card, then the next, then the last. Suits never count. Fully equal hands share the pot (but in a Show, the player who asked loses a tie, and in a Sideshow, the player who asked packs).

## Short stacks and the pot limit

**All-in.** If you can't pay a normal bet, you can go all-in: you put in all your chips and stay in the hand. You can win the pot as it is at that moment. Bets made after that go into a side pot that only the players still betting can win. If a second player goes all-in later, they can win the pot as it is then, and a new side pot starts. When at most one player can still bet, every hand still in turns over, and the best hand that can win each pot takes it. With 2 players left, going all-in turns both hands over at once. If only you put chips in a side pot, and only you can win it, those chips come back to you.

A stack smaller than the boot puts in what it has and is all-in from the start.

**Pot limit.** When the pot reaches 256 times the boot, every hand still in turns over, and the best hand wins. At a boot of 10, that is a pot of 2,560.

## Winning and scoring

The last player with chips wins. With a time limit, the hand in progress finishes when time is up, and the biggest stack wins.

**Placing.** Players who run out of chips rank by when they went out. Two out in the same hand: whoever started that hand with more chips ranks higher. The results screen shows "4,200 chips" for players still in and "out 3rd" for the rest.

**Boot levels:** 10, 20, 30, 50, 80, 100, 150, 200, 300, 500, then the boot doubles each level.

## Settings

The host picks these in the lobby.

| Setting | Options | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting chips | 500, **1,000**, 2,000 | 50, 100 or 200 times the first boot of 10. |
| Boot speed | Slow (10 hands), **Normal (6)**, Fast (4) | Hands per boot level. Counted in hands, not minutes. |
| Ending | **Last player left**, 20, 30 or 45 minutes | The time limits end after the hand in progress. Biggest stack wins. |
| Turn timer | 20, **30** or 45 seconds | Time for your turn. When it runs out, you pack. |

## On your screen

- **Top line.** Hand number, the boot, and the next boot with how many hands until it comes. With a time limit, the minutes left.
- **Status.** Whose turn it is, or "Your turn · You are Blind". During a Sideshow, who asked whom.
- **Seats.** Everyone else, from your left: name, chips, cards (face down, or face up after a show), a **Blind** or **Seen** tag, the last bet ("Chaal 40"), or Packed, All-in or Out. D marks the dealer. Whoever is playing is lit up, with their clock along the bottom.
- **The table.** The pot, the stake with the Blind and Seen prices, the pot limit, and main and side pots when there are any. After a show, each hand with its name, and who won what.
- **Your dock.** Your 3 cards (face down until you tap See), your hand's name ("Pair of Kings"), your chips, Blind or Seen, and your clock. Cards you saw in a Sideshow stay here for the rest of the hand.
- **Buttons.** See, Pack, Chaal, Double, and Show or Sideshow when you can. When someone asks you for a Sideshow: **No** and **Yes, compare**.
- Sounds and vibration cue the deal, bets, your turn and the pot. Your clock ticks in its last five seconds.

## When things go sideways

- **Running out of time.** You pack. Two in a row and you are sitting out: your hands pack at once (you still put in the boot) until you tap **I'm back**.
- **Disconnected player.** They pack on their turn and say no to a Sideshow, so the table never waits. They are back by themselves when they reconnect.
- **Idle player.** The host's Skip does what the clock would: a turn packs, a Sideshow answer is no.
- **The host removes a player.** Mid-hand, their cards pack. What they put in stays in the pot; the rest of their chips leave with them. A Sideshow they were in is called off. If only one player is left, they win.
- **Game over.** The host sees Rematch and Back to lobby; everyone else sees Play again.

## Tips

- Blind bets cost half. Staying Blind for a while is cheap, but you are betting without knowing.
- A Sideshow can push out the player before you, but you show them your cards too.
- With a short stack, an all-in can win the main pot even after everyone else keeps betting.
