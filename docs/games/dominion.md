# Dominion

Explore a hidden world, grow cities, research, and conquer your rivals. Turn-based, takes a while. 1–8 players.

One person can play alone: the game adds a computer opponent. Computers can fill empty seats too, up to 8 empires in total.

## The short version

- You start with one city and one infantry on a map you can't see. Explore, grow, research, fight.
- Turns are one at a time, around the table. On yours you move units, train, develop land and research, then End turn.
- Your cities pay credits at the start of each of your turns. Credits buy everything.
- Win by conquest (last empire standing), by score at the round limit, or by holding every capital, depending on the Victory setting.
- Combat has no dice. You see the exact damage before you commit.

## How it plays

1. **Start.** Everyone gets a people (see Peoples), a capital with one unit, and 5 credits (Citadel keepers get 3). Turn order follows the seats, and computers play after people. The map is hidden: you see around your units and inside your borders.
2. **Your turn begins.** Your cities pay out. Units get their movement back, and anything poisoned or chilled feels it.
3. **Do things, in any order:**
   - Move and attack with units.
   - Develop tiles inside your borders (harvest, build, roads). This grows the city that owns the tile.
   - Train units in your cities.
   - Research technologies.
   - Pick a reward when a city levels up.
   - Open Diplomacy.
4. **End turn.** Hit End turn, or let the clock run out. Then the next empire plays.
5. **Rounds.** A round is one pass through everyone. The top bar shows "Round 12 of 60".

The first-steps card in the top left walks you through eight things to try: explore, harvest, grow a city, research, train a unit, win a fight, take a village, and cross water. It ticks them off as you do them. Hide it with the x; the Guide (?) brings it back.

## Winning and scoring

**Victory** is a setting:

| Victory | You win when |
| --- | --- |
| Conquest | You're the last empire (or team) with a city. If a round limit is set and nobody's finished it by then, highest score wins. |
| Score | The round limit is reached. Highest score wins. |
| Hold the capitals | You own every original capital at the end of your turn, and still own them at the start of your next. Your allies' capitals count as yours. |

**Out of the game.** Lose every city and you're out; your units vanish. Surrendering (in Diplomacy) does the same, except your cities stay standing with no owner, up for grabs like villages.

**Score** is what ranks the results and breaks the round-limit finish:

- 100 per city level
- 50 per Park reward
- 25 per technology
- 5 per tile explored
- 10 per kill
- 100 per monument earned
- Temple culture on your land

**Ties** on score go to whoever owns more original capitals, then the higher total city levels, then more credits. If those are level too, the win is shared. With teams, members' totals are added together.

The results list shows each empire's points, with "fell" next to anyone who was eliminated. If the game saved a replay, the End turn button becomes **Watch replay** ("Turn by turn, fog lifted"): left and right arrows step, space plays or pauses, Esc exits.

## Settings

Host picks these in the lobby.

| Setting | Options | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Computers | **None**, 1–7 | Computer empires added on top of the people. Total empires are capped at 8, and a lone person always gets one computer. |
| Computer skill | Easy, **Normal**, Hard | Only shown when computers are playing. |
| Victory | **Conquest**, Score, Hold the capitals | See above. The lobby also gives a rough length estimate. |
| Map | **Auto**, 16×16, 24×24, 32×32 | Auto picks 16×16 for 1–2 empires, 24×24 for 3–5, 32×32 for more. Every empire needs 48 tiles; a size too small for the room is bumped up. |
| Terrain | **Continents**, Landmass, Lakes, Archipelago | Map shape. Continents and Archipelago can put rivals across water, so Sailing matters. |
| Turn clock | Off, 1 min, 2 min, **3 min**, 5 min | Time per turn. Computers have no clock. |
| Rounds | 30, **60**, 90, Unlimited | Round limit. Unlimited is only offered for Conquest; a Score game set to unlimited is bumped to 60. |
| Fog | **On**, Terrain shown, Off | On: you map only what you've seen. Terrain shown: the terrain is known from the start, but what's on it still needs eyes. Off: you see everything. |
| Resources | Sparse, **Standard**, Abundant | How much fruit, game, fish, crops and ore the map has. |
| Factions | **Mixed**, or one specific people | Mixed deals each empire a random people. Pick one and everyone plays it. |
| Teams | **Off**, 2, 3, 4 | Seats are dealt into teams in turn order. Needs at least that many empires, or it falls back to no teams. |
| Team vision | **Shared**, Own only | Only shown with teams. Whether allies see what each other sees. |

## On your screen

The map fills the screen. Everything else floats on top.

- **Top left:** your credits and what your cities pay ("+7 a turn"), your Score, monuments waiting to be placed, the round, and a list of empires. A CPU tag marks computers; T1, T2 mark teams.
- **Banner:** "Your turn", or "<Name> is playing" ("thinking" for a computer). With a clock it shows minutes and seconds, red at 20 seconds or less, with one warning sound when you're low.
- **Bottom left:** Research and Diplomacy. Diplomacy shows a badge when someone's offering peace.
- **Bottom right:** End turn, and how many units can still act. If units are idle or a city reward is waiting, the first press asks ("End anyway"). A pending reward you leave takes the first option.
- **Side buttons:** zoom in, zoom out, Next unit, Guide (?), and full screen on phones.
- **Inspector:** tap a tile and a panel shows the tile, the unit on it, the city, and what you can develop there. Tap the x to close it.

## Units and movement

- A unit moves in eight directions. Terrain costs: plains 1, forest 2, mountain 2 (needs Climbing), shallow water 1, ocean 1 (ships need Navigation), ice 1. Roads between roads and cities in friendly land cost half. A unit that hasn't moved yet can always take one step into a passable neighbour, even if it costs more than it has.
- **Zone of control.** Stepping next to an enemy fighter ends your move. Fliers, allies and treaty partners don't do this.
- **One unit per tile.** You can't stack.
- **Health and speed.** A unit shows its HP, Attack, Defense, Move and Range. Move is tiles per turn.
- **Capacity.** A city supports level + 1 units. Over that and you can't train there until it grows. Units without a home find one at the start of your turn.
- **Heal.** A unit that hasn't moved or fought can rest: +2 HP, or +4 on your own land. Resting also draws poison out.
- **Veterans.** After 3 kills a unit can be promoted: +5 max HP.
- **Disband** (needs Free Spirit) refunds half the cost, rounded down; converted units refund nothing.

### Combat

- Each side's strength is its attack (or defense) scaled by remaining health. Damage = 4.5 × attack × attacker strength ÷ (attacker strength + defender strength), rounded, at least 1. Nothing is random.
- A defender that survives hits back if the attacker is within the defender's range.
- **Defense bonuses** (only the best one counts): city ×1.5, city with Walls ×2, forest with Archery ×1.25, mountain with Meditation ×1.25.

### Water

- With Sailing, build a port on shallow water on your shore. A land unit that walks onto your port boards a free transport. Landing again ends its turn. The unit and ship share one health pool.
- Refit a transport that hasn't acted into a ship, same tile:

| Ship | Cost | Atk/Def | Move | Range | Needs | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Transport | free | 0/1 | 2 | 1 | Sailing | |
| Scout ship | 5 | 2/1 | 3 | 2 | Sailing | |
| Rammer | 8 | 3/3 | 3 | 1 | Navigation | |
| Bomber | 12 | 4/2 | 2 | 3 | Navigation | Also hits enemies beside the target for half. |

- Open ocean needs Navigation. Two ports on the same water link their cities like a road.

## Cities and land

- **Income.** Each city pays its level, +1 if it's your capital, +1 if it's linked to your capital by road or harbours, +1 for a Workshop, plus Markets. An enemy unit standing in a city stops its pay and its training.
- **Growth.** Harvesting and building on a city's tiles adds population. A city levels up when its population reaches level + 1. Bigger cities pay more and hold more units.
- **Rewards.** Each level-up offers a pick of two. The first is the default if you end the turn without choosing. A popup appears for it: "Grew to level 3". "Decide later" puts it off for the turn.

| Level | Choice |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | **Workshop** (+1 income) or Scouts (reveal land around the city) |
| 3 | **Treasury** (+5 credits now) or Walls (units in the city defend ×2) |
| 4 | **Growth** (+3 population) or Borders (territory grows to radius 2) |
| 5 and up | **Park** (+50 score) or Champion (a 35 HP unit joins the city) |

- **Capturing.** Move a unit into a village or a rival city. Survive until your next turn, and with the unit unmoved, press Capture. A village becomes your city. A city changes hands. Units that can't capture are marked in the inspector.
- **Develop.** Tap a tile inside your borders to see what's possible. A unit standing on it doesn't block building unless it's an enemy.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Harvest fruit, game or fish | 2 | +1 | Gathering, Hunting or Fishing | Uses the resource up. |
| Farm | 5 | +2 | Farming | Plains with crops. |
| Lumber camp | 3 | +1 | Forestry | Forest. |
| Mine | 5 | +2 | Mining | Mountain with ore. |
| Road | 2 | 0 | Roads | Plains, forest. |

## Research

Open Research (R). A tech costs 3 + 3 × its tier, plus 2 for each city past your first. Philosophy takes a fifth off. Tiers are 1 to 3: tier 1 techs are roots, the rest need their parent.

| Branch | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gathering | Gathering: harvest fruit | Farming: farms. Strategy: defenders. | Construction: mills, demolish. Diplomacy: peace treaties, embassies, infiltrators. |
| Hunting | Hunting: harvest game | Forestry: lumber camps. Archery: archers, forest defense. | Mathematics: siege engines. Spirituality: temples. |
| Riding | Riding: cavalry | Roads: roads, city links. Free Spirit: disband, cavalry fall back. | Commerce: markets. Chivalry: knights. |
| Climbing | Climbing: mountains | Mining: mines. Meditation: mountain defense. | Metallurgy: swordsmen, forges. Philosophy: sages, cheaper research. |
| Fishing | Fishing: harvest fish | Sailing: ports, boarding, scout ships. | Navigation: open ocean, rammers, bombers. |

Each People starts with one tier-1 tech.

## Diplomacy

Open Diplomacy (D) to see everyone you've met. Empires meet when they see each other's units or cities.

- **Offer peace** needs Diplomacy, and you must have met them. An offer lapses after 2 rounds. Answers (Accept, Decline) work on anyone's turn.
- **Peace** means no attacking, no zones of control between you, no taking each other's cities, and no standing in each other's cities.
- **Break peace.** Two taps. The treaty ends and war opens at the start of the breaker's next turn.
- **Embassy.** 5 credits. +1 credit a turn while you're at peace, and a view of their capital.
- **Surrender** is at the bottom. Two taps; it can't be undone.

With teams, allies never fight, share the win, and (with Team vision on) see what each other sees. Treasuries and research stay separate.

## Milestones

Five goals each earn a monument: Grand library (research 10 technologies), Merchant arch (connect 5 cities to your capital), Compass tower (explore 80% of the map), Victory column (win 10 battles), Peace garden (keep peace with 3 empires for 5 rounds). A monument is placed on open land of yours: +3 population in that city, and +100 score when earned. The top bar counts monuments waiting to be placed.

## Map features

- **Villages.** Neutral. Capture one to found a city.
- **Ruins.** The first unit to step in finds something at random: 3 population for the nearest city, a free tier 1 or 2 tech, a free unit, a reveal of the surrounding area, or 10 credits. Stepping onto ruins ends that unit's move.
- **Beacons.** The first time an empire sees one, its capital gains 1 population.

## When things go sideways

- **Your clock runs out.** Your turn ends on its own. Unspent units stay where they are; an unchosen city reward takes the first option.
- **Someone drops or goes quiet.** Their clock keeps running, so a clock game moves on. With no clock, the host can nudge them, and can skip their turn after a minute or at once if they're disconnected.
- **Someone is removed.** The host can remove a disconnected player. Their empire isn't deleted: a computer at Normal skill keeps it going.
- **A computer plays.** Computers take their turn a few actions at a time so you can follow. They have no clock.
- **Everyone's out but one side.** The game ends at once with a conquest win. If the last two sides fall in the same moment, it's a draw.
- **Stale actions.** If you act on an earlier turn's screen, the action is refused ("That was for an earlier turn").
- **Play again.** A rematch is a fresh game: new map, new peoples if Mixed, and the first seat rotates.

## Tips

- Cities hold level + 1 units, so growth, not coin, is often what stops you training.
- The damage preview is exact. If it says you lose, you do.
- Capture takes a full turn of sitting still. Protect the unit.
- Peace pays: embassies earn credits, and the Peace garden wants three treaties.
